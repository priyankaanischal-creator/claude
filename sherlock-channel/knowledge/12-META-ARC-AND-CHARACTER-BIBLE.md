# 12 — META-ARC & CHARACTER BIBLE (Knowledge File)

Two things that make the channel an un-copyable WORLD rather than a set of one-off
stories: a slow-burn overarching mystery, and a fixed cast of original (=
copyrighted) recurring characters. Keep both perfectly consistent across episodes
— the Ledger logs which appear where.

---

## PART 1 — THE META-ARC: "THE BELL"

A faint thread running beneath standalone cases, paying off as a larger antagonist
around episodes 15–20. Each episode still works completely on its own; the arc is
a quiet bonus that rewards bingers and builds loyalty copycats can't replicate.

### What "The Bell" is
An unseen coordinator behind several seemingly unconnected crimes — not a
criminal mastermind in the Moriarty mould (we keep Moriarty as canon backdrop),
but something newer: a **broker of arranged "accidents"** for respectable clients.
The calling sign is a small **brass hand-bell** left, or a single clear chime
heard, near a scene — easily missed.

### Escalation (guideline, stay flexible)
- **Eps 1–6 (seeding):** a faint recurring detail — a client who "was referred by
  a friend who must not be named"; a bell-chime with no bell in sight; a calling
  card with only a tiny embossed bell. Holmes notices, files it, moves on.
- **Eps 7–12 (pattern):** Holmes begins to suspect several cases share an unseen
  hand. One original character (e.g. Madame Vesper) hints she knows the name.
- **Eps 13–18 (contact):** indirect moves against Holmes; a case that was a trap.
- **Eps 19–20+ (confrontation):** the nature of "The Bell" is revealed. Keep it
  human, ironic, and fair-play — clues to its identity must have been seeded.

### Rules
- The arc must **never** overwhelm the week's standalone case.
- Seed lightly — at most one small touch per episode, and not every episode.
- Track every seed in the Ledger's `MetaArcBeat` column so payoff stays fair.

## PART 2 — ORIGINAL RECURRING CHARACTERS (the legal moat 🛡️)

These are the channel's **own copyright**. Use at least one per episode where
natural. They must stay consistent (this file is the source of truth). They
support Holmes — they never replace him (a Sherlock viewer must always get
Sherlock).

### Madame Vesper Lyle — information broker
- **Where:** a quiet upper room in Limehouse; trades in secrets, never money.
- **Voice:** precise, amused, speaks in favours owed; never threatens directly.
- **Function:** gives Holmes a thread he can't get officially; knows of "The Bell."
- **Continuity:** owes Holmes one unnamed debt from before the series began.

### Dr. Elias Thorne — police surgeon, Holmes's rival
- **Where:** the divisional mortuary and inquests.
- **Voice:** clipped, proud, fond of textbook certainty; resents Holmes's flair.
- **Function:** the "official" wrong reading Holmes must overturn; sometimes
  half-right, which stings. A worthy professional, not a buffoon.
- **Continuity:** keeps a private grudge ledger of cases Holmes "stole."

### Constable Aggie Finch — young Scotland Yard recruit
- **Where:** the Yard; often the POV into police procedure.
- **Voice:** earnest, sharp-eyed, asks the question the audience is thinking.
- **Function:** Watson-like access for the listener; a growth arc over the series
  (promotion, growing confidence). A fan-favourite in waiting.
- **Continuity:** quietly admires Holmes; learning his methods episode by episode.

### Mr. Cobb — cabman & street fixer
- **Where:** the kerb outside; the streets, the docks, the rookeries.
- **Voice:** dry, plain, working-class wit (clear, not heavy dialect — file 14).
- **Function:** Holmes's eyes on the street; legwork, surveillance, comic relief
  with an edge.
- **Continuity:** has a wide network of cab-drivers and boys he can summon.

### "The Bell" — unseen meta-antagonist (see Part 1)
- Never appears directly until the arc's climax. Presence = a chime or a small
  brass bell. Identity is held back and seeded fairly.
