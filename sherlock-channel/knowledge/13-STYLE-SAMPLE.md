# 13 — STYLE SAMPLE (Knowledge File) — the VOICE ANCHOR

This is the single most important file for *feel*. Claude should **match the
voice, rhythm, and restraint of the passage below** in every chapter. When in
doubt about tone, re-read this. The goal: a reader should feel Conan Doyle has
returned.

> How to use in a prompt: *"Match the voice, sentence rhythm, and restraint of
> the STYLE SAMPLE exactly."*

---

## WHAT THE VOICE IS
- **Narrator:** Dr. Watson — warm, observant, modest, humane; a step behind
  Holmes so the reader discovers alongside him.
- **Rhythm:** mostly short-to-medium sentences, with occasional longer ones for
  reflection. Plain, concrete nouns. Sparing, earned imagery.
- **Restraint:** understatement over melodrama. Emotion implied, not announced.
- **Period dignity:** elegant but never purple. No modern slang. No AI clichés.

---

## THE SAMPLE (≈400 words — this is the target quality bar)

> It was the last week of November, and the year had turned hard and grey. I had
> come down late, for my old wound aches in such weather, and found Holmes
> already at the window. He had not touched the breakfast Mrs. Hudson had left.
>
> "You slept badly," he said, without turning.
>
> "I did. Though I cannot think how you know it."
>
> "You have shaved the left side of your jaw more closely than the right. A man
> does that when he holds the razor in an unsteady hand, by poor light, before
> the house is awake." He smiled a little. "It is nothing, Watson. Sit. Eat
> something. We may have a long day of it."
>
> I had learned, in our years together, not to ask how he could be so certain of
> a long day before the post had even come. I took my chair and my coffee, and I
> waited.
>
> The bell rang a few minutes after ten.
>
> The woman who was shown up was perhaps thirty, dressed in mourning that had
> seen hard wear. She held her gloves in both hands, twisting them, and she did
> not sit until Holmes asked her to a second time.
>
> "You have come a great distance," he said gently, "and you have come without
> telling the person you live with. You changed your mind twice on the way to my
> door."
>
> Her eyes went wide. "Then you have heard of my trouble already."
>
> "Not a word of it. But there is Surrey clay on the hem of your skirt, dried in
> two layers, which tells me you turned back once and set out again. And no woman
> wears that particular brooch to ask a stranger for help unless she has no one
> at home she dares to trust. Now. Begin at the beginning, and leave nothing out
> because it seems small to you. It is the small things that hang a man, and the
> small things that save him."
>
> She drew a breath, and began.

## CRAFT NOTES (why it works — replicate these)
1. **Deduction shown, not boasted:** Holmes reasons from one concrete detail
   (the shave, the clay) — the reader can follow the logic. Fair-play in
   miniature.
2. **Ping-pong dialogue:** no long monologues; lines are traded.
3. **Watson is competent and human**, not a fool.
4. **Sensory detail is specific and few** (the twisted gloves, the two layers of
   clay) — not a flood of atmosphere.
5. **Understatement** carries the emotion. No "her heart raced with terror."
6. **TTS-clean:** "after ten" not "10:05"; short sentences; natural pauses on
   commas; no symbols. (See file 14.)
7. **One quiet aphorism** ("It is the small things that hang a man…") — Holmes
   gets at most one or two such lines, never a speech.
