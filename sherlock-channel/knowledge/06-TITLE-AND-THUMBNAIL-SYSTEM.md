# 06 — TITLE & THUMBNAIL SYSTEM (Knowledge File)

Titles and thumbnails are generated AFTER the blueprint (you need to know the
story). Claude proposes options; the human picks/tweaks. Two hard rules:

> **HONESTY RULE:** The title/thumbnail must never promise something the episode
> doesn't deliver. A viewer who clicks for Sherlock must always get Sherlock.
> **VARIETY RULE:** Rotate the format. Never use the same format or the same
> opening word two videos in a row (log it in the Ledger).

---

## 7 TITLE FORMATS (rotate)

| # | Format | Example |
|---|--------|---------|
| 1 | **Classic literary** | *The Silent Passenger* · *The House That Listened* |
| 2 | **Impossible-premise statement** (strongest hook) | *A Man Vanished From a Train That Never Stopped* |
| 3 | **Question hook** | *Why Did the Dead Man's Watch Stop at Midnight?* |
| 4 | **Location + event** | *Murder at the Lighthouse* |
| 5 | **First-person intrigue** | *I Was Hired to Find a Man Who Did Not Exist* |
| 6 | **Time-pressure** | *Sixty Minutes to Name a Killer* |
| 7 | **Reversal / twist tease** | *Everyone Saw Him Die. No One Saw Him Killed.* |

### Title build rules
- After the blueprint, Claude offers **7–10 options** spanning ≥3 formats.
- Keep it readable on mobile; front-load the intriguing word.
- May append a quiet series tag for branding (e.g. *— From Watson's Dispatch
  Box*) — but the brand tag goes in the THUMBNAIL/series, not always the title.
- For Part 1/2 episodes: mark "(Part 1)" clearly.

### BANNED title words (overused YouTube-mystery clichés)
`enigma, unraveled, chilling, dark secret(s), you won't believe, shocking,
sinister truth, mind-blowing, the truth about, gone wrong, decoded`
(Avoid ALL-CAPS clickbait and excessive punctuation like "?!!".)

## THUMBNAIL CONCEPT SYSTEM (object-focused)

Visuals are produced in a separate project, so Claude's job is to suggest the
**concept** (in the Editorial Help file). The channel's signature thumbnail =
**ONE crucial object, close-up, dramatic light** — NOT a generic Holmes
silhouette. This is instantly recognizable and hard to copy.

### Per episode, Claude proposes:
1. **Hero object** — one evocative object from the case (a cracked monocle, a
   blood-flecked theatre ticket, a stopped clock face, a single chess piece).
2. **Mood/lighting** — e.g. "single warm lamp, deep shadow, cold blue edge."
3. **Optional 2–4 word overlay text** — short, punchy, NOT the full title
   (e.g. "WHO STOPPED THE CLOCK?"). Often best with NO text.
4. **Consistent frame element** — a recurring visual signature for brand
   recognition (e.g. a faint dispatch-box label, a consistent corner emblem,
   the same font for any overlay).

### Thumbnail rules
- One focal object. No clutter. No faces of real people.
- Keep a consistent color grade / font across episodes = brand identity.
- The object should appear in the actual story (honesty rule).
- Rotate the object type so thumbnails don't all look identical.
